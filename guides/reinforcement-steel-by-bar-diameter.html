Reinforcement steel by bar diameter: 10, 12, 16 and 20 mm

By Abdullah Mukter Jewel · Updated 2026-10-25

Steel is the largest single cost in the frame of a building, and it is bought by weight. Knowing the weight per metre of each diameter lets you check a bar schedule or a supplier's delivery.

Weight per metre

The unit weight of a round bar in kg per metre is the diameter in mm squared divided by 162 (about). This gives:

Diameterkg per metrekg per 12 m barBars per tonne
10 mm0.6177.4about 135
12 mm0.88810.7about 94
16 mm1.5818.9about 53
20 mm2.4729.6about 34

Where each size is used

  • 10 mm: stirrups and ties in columns and beams, and slab reinforcement in light work.
  • 12 mm: slab bars, secondary beam bars and stair reinforcement.
  • 16 mm: main bars in beams and columns for ordinary buildings.
  • 20 mm: heavily loaded columns, footings and the lower floors of taller buildings.

The final sizes and spacing always come from the structural engineer's drawings. They depend on loads, spans and the number of storeys.

Counting bars and wastage

Divide the weight needed by the weight of a 12 m bar to get the number of bars. Add wastage for cutting, laps and bending, usually 3 to 7%. Binding wire is about 1% of the steel weight.
